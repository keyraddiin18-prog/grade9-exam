1. Definition of Biology

Biology is the scientific study of life and living organisms.

The term Biology is derived from two Greek words:

  • Bios — meaning life
  • Logos — meaning study or science

Characteristics of Living Things

  1. Organization: Living organisms have a complex and organized structure, typically made up of cells.
  2. Response to Stimuli: Living organisms can respond to environmental stimuli or changes.
  3. Growth and Development: Living systems grow and develop according to instructions encoded in their DNA.
  4. Reproduction: Living organisms have the ability to reproduce and pass genetic information to the next generation.
  5. Heredity: Genetic traits are passed from one generation to another through DNA.
  6. Metabolism: Living organisms carry out chemical reactions to transform energy and maintain life.
  7. Homeostasis: Living organisms regulate their internal environment to maintain stable conditions.
  8. Adaptation through Evolution: Organisms can change over generations and become adapted to their environment.

2. What is a Scientific Study?

A scientific study is a systematic investigation aimed at discovering new knowledge, verifying existing knowledge, or solving specific problems.

Scientific studies use:

  • Observation
  • Experimentation
  • Data collection
  • Analysis
  • Evidence-based conclusions

3. Biology and Other Natural Sciences

Biology is closely related to other natural sciences. It combines information and methods from different fields to understand life.

Biology and Chemistry

Understanding elements, molecules and chemical reactions in living organisms requires principles of chemistry.

Biology and Physics

Biological processes such as photosynthesis involve concepts from both chemistry and physics.

Biology combines knowledge from different sciences to study living organisms at different levels.

4. Why Do We Study Biology?

Humans naturally seek to understand the world and living organisms.

  • To understand how organisms function.
  • To understand how organisms interact with their environment.
  • To develop medicines for diseases.
  • To advance genetics and cell biology.
  • To improve agriculture.
  • To investigate crimes through forensic biology.
  • To study ecosystems.
  • To understand environmental problems.
  • To produce useful products through fermentation.
  • To help in understanding diseases such as AIDS, tuberculosis and cancer.

5. The Scientific Method

The scientific method is a structured process used to investigate natural phenomena through observation, hypothesis formation, experimentation and analysis.

Biologists use the scientific method to investigate questions and develop scientific explanations.

Steps of the Scientific Method

1. Observation
Noticing and describing a phenomenon or problem.
2. Asking Questions
Formulating a clear and focused question.
3. Forming a Hypothesis
Proposing a tentative explanation or prediction that can be tested.
4. Testing the Hypothesis
Designing and conducting an experiment to test the hypothesis.
5. Data Collection
Gathering and recording observations or experimental results.
6. Analysis
Interpreting the collected data.
7. Conclusion
Drawing conclusions based on the analysis.
8. Communication
Sharing results with the scientific community for review and possible replication.

8. The Light Microscope

A microscope is an instrument used to observe objects that are too small to be seen clearly with the naked eye.

Types of Microscopes

  • Light Microscope: Uses a beam of light to view specimens.
  • Electron Microscope: Uses electrons and electromagnets and provides much higher magnification.

Types of Light Microscope

  1. Simple Light Microscope: Consists of a single lens.
  2. Compound Light Microscope: Consists of two or more glass lenses.
The compound microscope is the most common microscope used in biology.

9. Parts and Functions of a Light Microscope

1. Eyepiece (Ocular)

Used to look through the microscope.

2. Eyepiece Tube

Holds the eyepiece.

3. Objective Lenses

Provide different levels of magnification.

4. Arm

Supports the microscope and connects major components.

5. Body Tube

Connects the eyepiece to the objective lenses.

6. Nosepiece

Rotating part that holds the objective lenses.

7. Adjustment Knobs

Used to focus the microscope.

  • Coarse adjustment: Brings the specimen into general focus.
  • Fine adjustment: Produces a sharper focus.
8. Stage

The platform on which the specimen is placed.

9. Stage Clip

Holds the glass slide in position.

10. Aperture

An opening in the stage that allows light to reach the specimen.

11. Microscopic Illuminator

The light source of the microscope.

12. Condenser

Collects and focuses light onto the specimen.

13. Diaphragm (Iris)

Controls the amount of light reaching the specimen.

14. Base

The bottom part that supports the microscope.

10. Handling and Using a Light Microscope

  1. Carry the microscope properly.
  2. Use a clean and dry slide.
  3. Never touch the lens surfaces.
  4. Place the slide in the middle of the stage.
  5. Always begin focusing with low power.
  6. Use the coarse adjustment first.
  7. Use fine adjustment to obtain a clear image.
  8. Lower the stage before removing the slide.
  9. Clean the microscope after use.
  10. Use lens paper to clean microscope lenses.
  11. Return the 4× objective over the stage.
  12. Turn off the microscope light.
  13. Return the microscope to its proper cabinet.
  14. Wrap the power cord correctly.

11. General Laboratory Safety Rules

Laboratory safety is important before, during and after laboratory activities.
  1. Follow laboratory instructions.
  2. Know the location of safety equipment.
  3. Dress appropriately for laboratory work.
  4. Never eat or drink in the laboratory.
  5. Never taste or sniff chemicals.
  6. Act responsibly in the laboratory.
  7. Keep the laboratory area clean.
  8. Store laboratory waste properly.
  9. Handle chemicals properly.
  10. Know what to do in case of laboratory accidents.
  11. Always give priority to safety and chemical hygiene.
